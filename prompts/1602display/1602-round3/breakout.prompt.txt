Design ONE clock face for a 16x2 HD44780 character LCD (a "1602") with the
1602-clock skill (.claude/skills/1602-clock). Read SKILL.md,
references/gotchas.md and references/design-guide.md first, and skim
references/worked-examples.md. Edit only sim/designs/breakout.js (already
registered as a placeholder; give it a real name and blurb). Don't touch other
designs, index.js or sim/js/. Always pass --out with a path starting
out/breakout when rendering, and don't render other designs. Work alone: don't
spawn subagents. Other agents are building other faces on this machine.

Starting idea (push past it, or replace it if you find something stronger):
Breakout / Arkanoid. The digits are walls of bricks in the top of the glass (cell-aligned brick tiles; the gaps between cells are the mortar), a paddle slides on the bottom row. The ball is always in play, bouncing with the slow glass's smear as its trail, and the paddle (an AI with personality: it nearly misses, panics, lunges) keeps it alive; the time's bricks are armoured until the minute. Minute: the armour drops, the ball goes into a frenzy and smashes exactly the bricks of the old digit that the new one doesn't need, then new bricks slide down from above to complete the new numeral. Hour: multiball and a falling power-up capsule.

GOAL: a stranger looks at this little green or blue strip and says "wait,
that's a clock?" Creativity comes first. A competent, conventional face is a
fail. This owner loves playful, character-driven, game-like faces (Pac-Man,
Pong, invaders, cuckoo went down well) and smooth, physical transitions, and
dislikes ordinary fonts, ordinary layouts and faces that sit still.

THE HARDWARE IS THE CANVAS. Eight CGRAM tiles at once, 5x8 dots each, a
glass that takes 150-200 ms to switch, gaps between cells, ROM characters
for free. Great 1602 faces turn those limits into the idea: the seams become
the cracks between bricks, the slow glass becomes a glow or a ghost, ROM
characters become props and scenery.

REQUIREMENTS
1. Concept: the time is expressed by the theme's own mechanism. Test: if
   swapping your digits for the plain ROM digits still works, the digits are
   not part of the idea yet.
2. Letterforms: don't use the one-dot-thin ROM digits for the main time. Draw
   a face that belongs to the theme (a kit of cell-aligned parts, custom
   tiles, or the time made of the world's objects). Any small labels use ROM
   only if it fits the look.
3. Layout: not a centred HH:MM line. Use all 32 cells as a stage, and use
   both rows. Hours and minutes may be different systems.
4. Always alive: something moves, at a pace the glass can show, every second,
   not only at the turn.
5. Minute turn: the old time physically becomes the new one through the
   theme's process, with every visible step at least ~150 ms. It must read
   mid-transition and never show a wrong time. The hour gets a bigger,
   distinct show.
6. Characters, if any, are carefully hand-drawn 5x8 tiles with personality
   (anticipation, reactions, idle fidgets) and they act on the time.
7. Technical: frame() is a pure function of time; `node tools/tile-budget.mjs
   --design breakout` passes (never more than 8 tiles, no trap #1); frame cost
   fits the declared bus (default i2c100); fps no higher than the glass can
   use (~10-12). Legible at 1:1. No hidden extra features.

PROCESS
1. Pitch three very different concepts, none a reskin of an existing design
   (`node tools/render.mjs --list`). For each, count the tiles it needs at the
   worst minute of the day before choosing. Pick the strongest, judging
   harshly.
2. Glyphs first: draw the digits and characters, render them, and iterate
   until each reads at 1:1 and looks designed.
3. Build, then render with the LC lag on:
   out/breakout.png (--scale 14 --crop view --style clean),
   out/breakout-hero.png (--style photo --crop body),
   out/breakout-turn.png (--sheet across 10:09:59),
   out/breakout-hour.png (--sheet across 10:59:58),
   out/breakout.gif (a minute turn) and out/breakout-hour.gif. Look at every image.
4. Ask: is the font, layout, motion or transition ordinary? Is it charming
   and surprising? Fix the weakest part and re-render. At least two rounds.
5. Report: the idea in one sentence, render paths, the tile-budget result
   (worst slot count, worst frame cost), and the honest weakest point.

Lessons from the last two 1602 rounds (20 faces), from the owner's gallery:
- Strongest stills: pigeons (crumb numerals on a ledge, birds beside them),
  juggler (four digits on a juggling arch over both rows), zipper (bold).
- Failures to avoid:
  - character parts beside the digits that read as letters or digits: fish
    tails read as "M" and heads as arrows, so "<18M <56M" looked like text;
    window boxes read as "00000". Check what every non-digit cell spells.
  - a wall of solid 0xFF blocks with negative digits cut in it reads as a
    black slab; the digits got lost. Keep plenty of empty glass.
  - one-cell-wide digits look sparse and weak next to a scene; a small
    classic "18:56" with the scene beside it is a fail.
  - an hour shown as a kanji or a symbol reads as a little figure; the time
    must be readable by any stranger.
  - long gaps: a turn that leaves a digit empty for 12-30 s; keep empty
    stretches to a few seconds.
  - a beam/stripe effect laid over the digits hid the hour.
- tile-budget samples only 11 instants per minute: run your own dense frame
  sweep across the turn and the hour (every frame must fit 8 tiles and show
  a right time). Check a night and a noon clock and all ten digits in every
  slot. Decide 12 or 24 h deliberately; 18:56 should not read as 6:56 unless
  there is an AM/PM cue.

LOOKING AT YOUR RENDERS: on this machine the Read tool often returns nothing
for PNG/GIF files (no image arrives). If an image does not come back, do not
guess: check it as text instead. `node tools/frame.mjs breakout --clock HH:MM:SS --t <s>` prints the emulator's dots as ASCII.
Never claim you looked at an image you did not actually see; say in your
report which checks were visual and which were text.
