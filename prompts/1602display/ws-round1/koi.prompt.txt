Design ONE clock face for shaped WS2812 RGB LED boards with the ws2812-clock
skill (.claude/skills/ws2812-clock): layout 'tri-ribbon', five triangles alternating: a long ribbon, one lattice (600),
188 x 52 mm, 600 LEDs, supply 4 A.

First read SKILL.md, references/panels.md and references/hardware.md.
Then read sim/designs/hive.js and neon.js for the API. They show the
mechanics; they are not the creative bar.
Then run `node tools/info.mjs --layout tri-ribbon` and
`node tools/render.mjs chain --layout tri-ribbon --room lit --out out/koi-boards.png`
to see the boards.

Edit only sim/designs/koi.js. It is already registered as a placeholder;
give it a real name and blurb, and keep layouts: ['tri-ribbon'] first. Don't
touch other designs, index.js, sim/js/ or tools/; put any helpers in your own
file. If you find a bug in the shared tools, report it, don't fix it.

Starting idea (push past it, or replace it if you find something stronger):
Koi stream. The long trapezoid ribbon is a stream seen from above: water caustics, lily pads drifting, koi (vermilion, gold, white with red patches) swimming. The koi school together and their bodies curve into the numerals: the digits are made of fish, scales glinting. Minute turn: a pebble or a crumb sends ripples; the fish of the changed digit scatter and regroup into the new one. Hour: a golden koi leaps up the waterfall at one end and becomes a dragon that sweeps along the ribbon (the koi-to-dragon legend), leaving the new hour. Characters drawn with care; legible at real size. The five boards share one continuous lattice.

GOAL: a stranger looks at it and says "wait, that's a clock?" Creativity comes
first. A competent, pretty, conventional face is a fail.

REQUIREMENTS
1. Concept: the time is expressed by the theme's own mechanism. Test: if
   swapping your digits for a plain bitmap font still works, the digits are
   not part of the idea yet.
2. Letterforms: draw your own, made of or carried by the theme's material,
   and designed for THIS lattice. Kit fonts (STROKE, COMB, SQ3X5) are not
   allowed for the time, not even as a skeleton with texture painted on.
   Design all ten digits as a set.
3. Use the shape. The outline, the lattice (triangular / square / rhombic),
   the hexagon's rings and the seams between boards are material for the
   idea; a face that would look the same on a rectangle has missed the
   point. Never the default of four equal digits in a row with decoration
   around them.
4. Always alive: smooth 60 fps motion that belongs to the theme, every
   second, not only at the turn. Positions glide between LEDs (kit.splat,
   distance fields), they don't hop.
5. Minute turn: the old digit physically becomes the new one through the
   theme's process, 0.8-3 s, eased, no pops or cuts. The time is never
   wrong mid-transition (empty or half-drawn is fine; a different number is
   not). The hour gets a bigger, distinct show, and the time stays readable
   through it (blank for more than ~1 s is a fail).
6. Colour: design a palette for the theme, not rainbow-everything. Light
   adds. Black is free and makes light glow. Below ~8/255 colours step and
   shift hue, so keep dark tones deliberate; use dither: true for slow dim
   fades.
7. Power: check.mjs must PASS the 4 A supply all day. Budget the
   light: dark grounds, lit strokes, saturated colours.
8. Look: choose bare, smoke or opal (+ gap) deliberately for the idea, and
   say why.
9. Real size: the LEDs are 4 mm apart and the whole face is 188 x 52
   mm, seen from 1-2 m. Judge legibility at --scale 3 (about true size on a
   monitor) as well as zoomed.
10. Technical:
    - frame() is a pure function of time: closed form, or deterministic
      caches keyed by the minute.
    - If you must simulate, warmup <= 2 s.
    - Dim with ctx.night.
    - `node tools/check.mjs --design koi` has no FAIL.
    - frame() mean under ~1 ms in Node.
    - No hidden extra features.

PROCESS
1. Pitch three very different concepts for this layout. Pick the strongest,
   judging harshly.
2. Digits first. Render every digit in every slot:
   node tools/render.mjs koi --clocks 00:00:30,11:11:30,22:22:30,03:33:30,04:44:30,05:55:30,16:06:30,17:17:30,18:18:30,19:39:30 --out out/koi-digits.png
   Iterate until each digit is beautiful and unmistakable.
3. Build, then render these, and look at every image:
   - out/koi.png
   - out/koi-turn.png: --clock 10:09:58 --frames 9 --duration 6 --sheet
   - out/koi-hour.png: the same at --clock 10:59:58, and once at 23:59:58
   - out/koi.gif: --clock 10:09:57 --frames 250 --duration 10 --gif --scale 5
   - out/koi-hour.gif
4. Critique it in the owner's voice. Is the font, layout, motion or
   transition ordinary? Is there a wow moment? Fix the weakest part and
   re-render. At least two rounds.
5. Report:
   - the idea in one sentence
   - the look chosen and why
   - render paths
   - the check.mjs result
   - the honest weakest point

THE OWNER'S TASTE (from ~90 faces they rated on the sibling OLED and 1602 skills)
- Loved:
  - scope, radar, sand: extraordinarily smooth, physical, fancy transitions
  - cube: 3D, constant dynamic
  - numberfolk: digits as cute characters
  - nixie
  - urwerk and ressence: "not digits anymore"
  - playful character and game designs (their avatar is a rainbow frog in
    round glasses)
- Disliked:
  - ordinary or kit fonts
  - four big digits with decoration
  - static between turns
  - abrupt transitions
  - elements that don't belong to one world
  - "good parts but no wow moment"
- They accept reduced legibility for real artistry, but the time must still
  be readable.
