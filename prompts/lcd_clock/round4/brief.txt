REQUIREMENTS
1. Concept: the time is expressed by the theme's own mechanism. Test: if
   swapping your digits for a plain font still works, the digits are not
   part of the idea yet.
2. Letterforms: if the time is shown as digits (it doesn't have to be),
   draw your own, made of or carried by the theme's material, designed for
   this pixel grid. The kit's fonts (strokeDigit, segDigit, dotText, 3x5,
   5x7) are not allowed for the time, not even as a skeleton with texture
   painted on. Design all ten digits as a set and make them beautiful at
   this resolution (hard pixels or AA, chosen per element, on purpose).
   Small secondary type (a date, a label), if any, must be in the same
   world's style too.
3. Use the canvas: a 2:1 strip, full colour, the backlight. Never the
   default of four equal digits in a row with decoration around them.
4. Always alive at 60 fps: smooth motion that belongs to the theme, every
   second, not only at the turn. Positions glide by fractions of a pixel
   (AA), they don't hop. Motion of ~0.3-2 px per frame reads smooth on this
   glass; the crystal's ~15 ms response is about one 60 fps frame, so fast
   high-contrast motion smears (renders show it; that is the real look).
5. Minute turn: the old digit physically becomes the new one through the
   theme's process, 0.8-3 s, eased, no pops or cuts. The time is never wrong
   mid-transition (empty or half-drawn is fine; a different number is not).
   The hour gets a bigger, distinct show, and the time stays readable
   through it (blank for more than ~1 s is a fail).
6. Colour: design a palette for the theme, say why in the header comment.
   RGB565 bands gradients unless dithered. It is an LCD: black is a dark
   grey that glows a little, so a mostly black face looks like a weak OLED;
   if your world is dark, make the dark itself deliberate (deep coloured
   darks, vignettes, glow). Dim with ctx.night through ctx.lcd.backlight,
   and check the face still reads at night.
7. 60 fps on a small MCU: fps: 60 means a 16.7 ms budget. Keep the rough C
   estimate for esp32c3 (no FPU, the weakest profile) at most ~14 ms in the
   worst frame of check.mjs, and the mean well under. Per-pixel callbacks
   (paint, fillWhere, fillSDF) over big areas are what blow it: precompute
   sprites, masks or distance fields per minute and look them up; use
   g.coarse for soft fields; kit.sin/cos, not Math.sin, in hot loops. No
   per-pixel allocation or closures in hot loops; keep hot objects
   monomorphic. Check tearing for fast horizontal motion of tall shapes
   (render --tear); driver: 'dirty' is an option if little changes.
8. Real size: the whole face is 21.7 x 10.8 mm seen from arm's length.
   Detail finer than ~2 px vanishes. Judge legibility at --scale 4 (about
   true size) as well as zoomed.
9. Technical:
   - frame() is a pure function of time: closed form, or deterministic
     caches keyed by the minute. If you must simulate, simulate from the
     start of the current minute inside frame, warmup <= 2 s of work.
   - `node tools/check.mjs --design {KEY}` passes (it takes ~40 s at 60 fps).
   - Work done outside the main canvas (a once-a-minute redraw into your
     own buffer) is invisible to the estimate: keep it small, or spread it
     over several frames, and state its rough cost in the report.
   - The kit's colour helpers (mix, dither565, ...) are billed as Math
     calls per use; in per-pixel loops use your own integer arithmetic.
   - Flash: precomputed tables and sprites should total <= ~256 KB on the
     device (RGB565 = 2 bytes a pixel); state the total in the report.
   - No hidden extra features.

PROCESS
1. Pitch three very different concepts in your theme's world. Pick the
   strongest, judging harshly.
2. Digits first. Render every digit in every slot:
   node tools/render.mjs {KEY} --clocks 00:00:30,11:11:30,22:22:30,03:33:30,04:44:30,05:55:30,16:06:30,17:17:30,18:18:30,19:39:30 --px 3 --cols 2 --out out/{KEY}-digits.png
   Iterate until each digit is beautiful and unmistakable.
3. Build, then render these, and look at every image:
   - out/{KEY}.png (and --scale 4 --out out/{KEY}-true.png)
   - out/{KEY}-turn.png: --clock 10:09:58 --frames 12 --duration 4 --sheet --px 3 --cols 3
   - out/{KEY}-hour.png: the same at --clock 10:59:58, and once at 23:59:58
   - out/{KEY}-day.png: --clocks 02:47:30,07:15:30,12:00:30,18:05:30 --px 3
   - out/{KEY}.gif: --clock 10:09:57 --frames 150 --duration 3 --gif --px 4
     (50 fps is the GIF limit), and out/{KEY}-hour.gif at 10:59:57
   Contact sheets hide pops: also render a dense sheet (--frames 25) of each
   transition before calling it smooth.
4. Critique it in the owner's voice. Is the font, layout, motion or
   transition ordinary? Is there a wow moment? Fix the weakest part and
   re-render. At least two rounds.
5. Report:
   - the idea in one sentence
   - render paths
   - the check.mjs result (incl. esp32c3 max/mean estimate)
   - the honest weakest point

LESSONS FROM ~120 FACES ON SIBLING SKILLS (1602, OLED, LED matrices)
- The most common weakness: at rest the face still reads as four plain
  digits with a themed background, and the wow lives only in the turns.
  Make the time at rest belong to the theme too: carried by objects,
  characters, light or material, or by a non-digit system (dials, counts,
  positions) that still reads clearly.
- The strongest stills put the time on a big, bold physical object of the
  theme (wagon cargo, wax candles, mahjong tiles, leather puppets) with the
  world alive around it.
- Characters below ~12 px read as blobs: draw heroes bold and simple
  (outlined, a few flat colours, clear eyes) and check them at --scale 4.
- Anything else that looks like a time, or any numeral-sized ring or stroke
  beside the digits, competes with the time: keep it clearly secondary.
- Background made of the same stuff as the digits competes with them: keep
  the ground quieter, give numerals a keyline or a contrast moat, one hue
  family for the time.
- Fixes needed in past rounds, check yours for the same: a limb resting
  over a digit made 0 read as 6; a night state made the hours vanish; a
  bright noon washed the digits out; 4 read as U; 9 read as 'a' in a squat
  slot; an effect that changes over the minute closed a counter late in
  the minute (check stills at :05, :30 and :55); an hour show ran digits
  backwards and showed a wrong number for half a second (check dense
  sheets across the hour).
- Don't add the owner's avatar or any recurring mascot. Characters, if any,
  belong to the theme.
- Round 1 on this panel (shanshui, jelly, mitosis, vector) all passed, and
  these are the fixes I still had to send back. Check yours for the same:
  - All four still read, at rest, as HH then MM left to right (staggered
    depth and size helped, but the wow lived in the turns). Push harder on
    a composition where the time sits inside the world's own arrangement.
  - The transition's agent must belong to the world's style: a fluffy
    white cartoon cumulus in an ink-painted scroll looked pasted on.
  - Hero objects must be recognisable at --scale 4 by a stranger: the
    dragon read as an eel, diatoms as pills, a copper jelly mould as matte
    organ pipes. Give each its defining silhouette parts and material
    (horns and claws; ribs; a polished specular streak).
  - Two of four failed at night: muddy colours on near-black, or the far
    digits vanishing. Design the night palette on purpose, and render
    02:47:30 at --scale 4 with night dimming.
  - When all four digits change at midnight, keep any blank under ~1 s.
- Rounds 2 and 3 (12 faces) all passed; the fixes I still had to send:
  - Digits that read as LETTERS: a peaked 4 read as "A" (17:46 read as
    "17 A6"); check 4/A, 7/T or ?, 1/I or !, 0/O is fine. A 6 or 0 whose
    counter fills in reads as 8.
  - Anything that is not a digit but forms a stroke near one reads as an
    extra digit: stray fish next to "47" made "i47". Keep loose actors
    away from the digits or clearly dimmer and smaller.
  - Snapping numerals to a coarse grid of the theme's units (10 px
    creature tiles) filled the counters and made 6 read 8 and 09 read B8.
    Draw each digit as a clean shape with open counters first, then let
    the material fill or clip to it.
  - Heroes drawn as glyphs with sticks attached (cave animals as digits
    with fork legs and knob heads) don't read: give them bulk, a defining
    silhouette and their real material.
  - Material-made digits on a ground of the same saturation (gems on a
    board of gems, white dough on pale regolith) read by outline only:
    calm the ground, add a keyline or dark moat, and shade the counters.
  - The strongest of those 12 (pcb, topiary, artillery, racetrack, mochi)
    put the time on one big bold physical object of the world, gave it
    a distinct look per material, and kept the ground quieter.
- Keep sim/designs/{KEY}.js importable at all times: every tool loads
  index.js, which imports every design, so a half-written file breaks
  the other agents' renders too. Make each edit leave the file valid. If
  your renders fail because another design is mid-edit, wait a minute
  and retry; don't touch their file.

THE OWNER'S TASTE
- Loved: scope, radar, sand (extraordinarily smooth, physical, fancy
  transitions); cube (3D, constant dynamic); numberfolk (digits as cute
  characters); nixie; urwerk and ressence ("not digits anymore"); playful
  character and game designs.
- Disliked: ordinary or kit fonts; four big digits with decoration; static
  between turns; abrupt transitions; elements that don't belong to one
  world; "good parts but no wow moment".
- They accept reduced legibility for real artistry, but the time must
  still be readable.
