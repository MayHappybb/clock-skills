THIS ROUND, IN ORDER OF WEIGHT:
1. FLUENT TRANSITIONS are the centrepiece. The minute turn and the hour
   are one continuous physical process of the theme, like a master
   animator's work: anticipation, eased acceleration and settle, overlap
   and follow-through (parts start and finish at staggered times), arcs
   rather than straight lines. No pops, cuts, cross-fades of whole digits,
   or "old vanishes, pause, new appears". Every moving thing glides at
   about 1-2 px per frame at most; nothing jumps between consecutive
   frames. Prove it: map the turn every 1/60 s around its busiest moments
   (frame.mjs --map --crop over a changing digit at consecutive
   frames) and look for any pixel run that jumps.
2. THE FONT is drawn for this theme: its material, its construction, its
   way of bending; all ten digits designed as a set at this pixel grid.
3. FASCINATING overall design: one world, one bold idea, a "wait, that's a
   clock?" moment.
4. VIVID AND DYNAMIC: rich deliberate colour and the time itself in motion
   every second (see below).

THE TIME IS ALIVE. What the owner loved most (rubberhose, 1-bit): the
numerals ARE the theme's characters or material, they move every second on
a beat, and the turn happens through the theme's own action. So the TIME
ITSELF is in visible motion every second of the minute, at rest:
- The numerals are made of, or carried by, something that moves: they
  bounce, dance, flow, pulse, wave, sway, are crossed by travelling fronts,
  or are characters that act.
- The world around them is busy too, with big moves, but calmer than the
  digits.
- A rhythm: a beat once a second (or twice) on top of continuous motion,
  plus a bigger flourish every ~10-15 s, so the minute has a structure.
- Readability still wins: at EVERY frame the time reads correctly at
  --scale 4. Motion must never make a digit read as another digit (no
  rotation that mirrors or narrows it, no part that closes a counter or
  opens a bowl). Keep the silhouette steady enough to read; surface and
  edges alive.
- Measure it: from the skill directory run
    node ../../../prompts/round9/alive.mjs airdancer

  changing within a second) and "ink" (% of non-ground pixels in the
  central area changing within a second; ground = commonest colour, a rough
  proxy in colour). Targets: second >= ~30% and ink >= ~40% at every rest
  moment. The number is not the judge: say what the digits do each second.
- Read sim/designs/xray.js (every pixel from material maps through a

Starting idea. It is short on purpose: invent the details yourself (the
palette, the font, the life at rest, the beat, the turn, the hour). Push
past it, or replace it with something stronger in its world:
THEME: inflatable tube men (wacky waving air dancers) outside a car lot under bunting. Each numeral is an air dancer whose fabric tube bends into the digit's shape, with a silly face and flailing arms at the free ends, each in its own vivid nylon colour. At rest ripples run up every tube and they flail on the beat. Minute turn: the old dancer's blower cuts out and it sags and folds to the ground, then the new one inflates up into its digit with a whoosh. Hour: all four go wild with streamers and balloons while the time keeps reading.

GOAL: a stranger looks at it and says "wait, that's a clock?" Creativity
comes first. A competent, pretty, conventional face is a fail.

REQUIREMENTS
1. Concept: the time is expressed by the theme's own mechanism. Test: if
   swapping your digits for a plain font still works, the digits are not
   part of the idea yet.
2. Letterforms: draw your own, made of or carried by the theme's material
   or characters, designed for this pixel grid. The kit's fonts
   (strokeDigit, segDigit, dotText, 3x5, 5x7) are not allowed for the time,
   not even as a skeleton with texture painted on. Design all ten digits as
   a set, every edge designed. Small secondary type, if any, is in the same
   world's style.
3. Use the canvas: a 2:1 strip, the whole 160x80, the backlight. Never the
   default of four equal digits in a row with decoration around them.
4. Always alive at 60 fps: smooth motion that belongs to the theme, every
   second. The crystal's ~15 ms response is about one 60 fps frame, so fast
   high-contrast motion smears (renders show it; that is the real look).
5. Minute turn: the old digit physically becomes the new one through the
   theme's process, 0.8-3 s, eased, no pops or cuts. The time is never wrong
   mid-transition (empty or half-drawn is fine; a different number is not).
   The hour gets a bigger, distinct show, and the time stays readable
   through it (blank for more than ~1 s is a fail).
6. Colour: a deliberate palette; say why in the header comment. The time is
   the strongest contrast on the glass, the ground calmer. It is an LCD:
   black is a dark grey that glows a little.
7. 60 fps on a small MCU: a 16.7 ms budget. Keep the rough C estimate for
   esp32c3 (no FPU, the weakest profile) at most ~14 ms in the worst frame
   of check.mjs, and the mean well under. Per-pixel callbacks (paint,
   fillWhere, fillSDF) and gradients over big areas are what blow it:
   precompute sprites, masks or distance fields per minute and look them
   up; kit.sin/cos, not Math.sin, in hot loops. No per-pixel allocation or
   closures in hot loops. Check tearing for fast horizontal motion of tall
   shapes (render --tear).
8. Real size: the whole face is 21.7 x 10.8 mm seen from arm's length.
   Detail finer than ~2 px vanishes. Judge legibility at --scale 4.
9. Technical:
   - frame() is a pure function of time: closed form, or deterministic
     caches keyed by the minute. If you must simulate, simulate from the
     start of the current minute inside frame, warmup <= 2 s of work.
   - `node tools/check.mjs --design airdancer` passes (~40 s at 60 fps), and
     alive.mjs meets the targets.
   - RAM: working buffers at most ~150 KB beyond the 25 KB frame buffer;
     state the peak in the report.
   - Work done outside the main canvas (a once-a-minute redraw into your
     own buffer) is invisible to the estimate: keep it small or spread it
     over several frames, and state its rough cost.
   - The kit's colour helpers (mix, dither565, ...) are billed as Math
     calls per use; in per-pixel loops use your own integer arithmetic.
   - Flash: precomputed tables and sprites <= ~256 KB on the device; state
     the total.
   - No hidden extra features.

PROCESS
1. Pitch three very different concepts in your theme's world. Pick the
   strongest, judging harshly.
2. Digits first. Render every digit in every slot:
   node tools/render.mjs airdancer --clocks 00:00:30,11:11:30,22:22:30,03:33:30,04:44:30,05:55:30,16:06:30,17:17:30,18:18:30,19:39:30 --px 3 --cols 2 --out out/airdancer-digits.png
   and read each digit in the text maps. Iterate until each digit is
   beautiful and unmistakable.
3. Build, then render these (and check them through text maps):
   - out/airdancer.png (and --scale 4 --out out/airdancer-true.png)
   - out/airdancer-turn.png: --clock 10:09:58 --frames 12 --duration 4 --sheet --px 3 --cols 3
   - out/airdancer-hour.png: the same at --clock 10:59:58, and once at 23:59:58
   - out/airdancer-day.png: --clocks 02:47:30,07:15:30,12:00:30,18:05:30 --px 3
   - out/airdancer.gif: --clock 10:09:57 --frames 150 --duration 3 --gif --px 4
     (50 fps is the GIF limit), and out/airdancer-hour.gif at 10:59:57
   Contact sheets hide pops: map dense steps through each transition
   before calling it smooth and correct.
4. Critique it in the owner's voice. Is the transition truly fluent, or
   does anything pop? Is the font, layout or motion ordinary? Do the digits
   themselves move every second? Is it vivid? Is there a wow moment? Fix
   the weakest part and re-check. At least two rounds.
5. Report:
   - the idea in one sentence
   - what the digits do each second at rest, the flourish, the turn, the
     hour, and how you verified the turn has no jumps
   - render paths
   - the check.mjs result (incl. esp32c3 max/mean estimate), the alive.mjs
     result, flash and RAM totals
   - the honest weakest point

LESSONS FROM EARLIER ROUNDS
- At rest the face still read as four plain digits with a themed
  background: make the time belong to the theme, carried by its objects,
  characters or material.
- The strongest stills put the time on a big, bold physical object of the
  theme, with the world alive around it.
- Characters below ~12 px read as blobs: draw heroes bold and simple, with
  their defining silhouette parts big, and check at --scale 4.
- Anything else that looks like a time, or a numeral-sized ring or stroke
  beside the digits, competes with the time. Loose actors near the digits
  read as extra digits ("i47"): keep them away or clearly smaller/dimmer.
- Background of the same stuff and saturation as the digits competes with
  them: calm the ground, give numerals a keyline or a 3-4 px moat.
- Digits that read as LETTERS: a peaked 4 read "A", 4 read "U", 9 read "a"
  in a squat slot, 7/T, 1/I. A 6 or 0 whose counter fills reads as 8. Every
  counter needs at least ~4x6 px of ground. Draw each digit as a clean
  shape with open counters first, then let the material fill or clip to it.
- A limb or actor resting over a digit made 0 read as 6; an effect that
  changes over the minute closed a counter late (check :05, :30 and :55).
- A digit that rotates or turns while shown read as another (0 edge-on
  read 1).
- Partial digits read as other digits: a top-down wipe left an 8's lower
  loop (read 0); a 9 whose bowl came first read 0; a thinning 0 read "c";
  a 1 removed before the 9 beside it made 10:19 read 10:09. Remove every
  changing digit on the same schedule, evenly, or reveal the whole outline
  first, then fill. Removal must thin a digit or carry its material away,
  never close its open sides.
- Work changing digits concurrently; keep the unreadable window under ~1 s,
  and plan the midnight four-digit change first.
- Never move the whole time across a screen edge (an hour scroll showed
  "4·59" at 14:59:59).
- A shape dashing at ~8 px a frame smeared and tore: keep a shape's own
  motion near 1-2 px/frame.
- Night failures: muddy colours on near-black, far digits vanishing.
- Particles carrying the time: give each a home position precomputed per
  minute and derive its motion in closed form from time and index
  (kit.noise, kit.sin), so any frame can be built cold.
- Don't add the owner's avatar or any recurring mascot.
- Keep sim/designs/airdancer.js importable at all times: every tool loads
  index.js, which imports every design, so a half-written file breaks the
  other agents' renders too. If renders fail because another design is
  mid-edit, wait a minute and retry; don't touch their file.
