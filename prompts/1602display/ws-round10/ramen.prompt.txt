Design ONE clock face with the ws2812-clock skill (.claude/skills/ws2812-clock):
layout 'hub', one HUB75 64x64 P2 panel (4096), 128 x 128 mm, 4096 LEDs, supply 4 A.

This round is for the HUB75 panel hub64, alone or assembled (read SKILL.md's
"HUB75 panels" section and the hub64 parts of references/panels.md and
references/hardware.md): a HUB75 RGB matrix, 64x64 pixels on 2 mm (128 mm
square), black GOB LEDs. Panels butt with no seam, so an assembly is one
bigger raster; non-rectangular assemblies have -1 in the raster where a
panel is missing (nothing is drawn there). It is a real pixel canvas:
scenes, characters with faces, real typography, 3D are all possible. The
owner asked for "maximum creativity": interesting themes, amazing smooth and
fluent motion (both the constant life and the transitions), and interesting
fonts that fit the theme. Your assembly's size and SHAPE are part of the
brief: design for them, not a 64x64 idea stretched.

Starting idea (push past it, or replace it if you find something stronger):
A steaming bowl of ramen seen from above on the 64x64 panel: rich broth with glints of oil, a soft egg, nori sheets, scallions, chashu, a naruto fishcake swirl. The time is in the bowl: the noodles are lifted by chopsticks into the shapes of the numerals, or the hour is brushed on the nori and the minutes are noodles. Always alive: steam curls up (soft, translucent), oil droplets drift and merge on the broth, the broth ripples. Minute turn: chopsticks dip in and lift the old digit's noodles away in a long slurp, and new noodles are lowered and settle into the new numeral. Hour: a ladle of fresh broth swirls everything into a whirlpool and the toppings resettle.

GOAL: a stranger looks at it and says "wait, that's a clock?" Creativity comes
first. A competent, pretty, conventional face is a fail.

REQUIREMENTS
1. Concept: the time is expressed by the theme's own mechanism. Test: if
   swapping your digits for a plain bitmap font still works, the digits are
   not part of the idea yet.
2. Letterforms: if the time is shown as digits (it doesn't have to be), draw
   your own, made of or carried by the theme's material, and designed for
   THIS pixel grid. Kit fonts (STROKE, COMB, SQ3X5) and generic 3x5 / 5x7 /
   8x8 pixel fonts are not allowed for the time, not even as a skeleton with
   texture painted on. Design all ten digits as a set, and make them
   beautiful at this resolution (anti-aliasing by coverage is available:
   linear light in ctx.out).
3. Use the board. On HUB75: the size of the canvas, its square shape (or
   the assembly's shape), the black GOB face. On the 8x8 tiles: their
   smallness, the seams between tiles, one MCU per tile. Never the default
   of four equal digits in a row with decoration around them.
4. Always alive: smooth 60 fps motion that belongs to the theme, every
   second, not only at the turn. Positions glide between pixels (coverage,
   sub-pixel rendering), they don't hop.
5. Minute turn: the old digit physically becomes the new one through the
   theme's process, 0.8-3 s, eased, no pops or cuts. The time is never
   wrong mid-transition (empty or half-drawn is fine; a different number is
   not). The hour gets a bigger, distinct show, and the time stays readable
   through it (blank for more than ~1 s is a fail).
6. Colour: design a palette for the theme, not rainbow-everything. Light
   adds. Black is free and makes light glow. Below ~8/255 colours step and
   shift hue, so keep dark tones deliberate; use dither: true for slow dim
   fades. HUB75 panels are very bright indoors: brightness 0.25-0.5.
7. Power and heat: check.mjs must have no FAIL on the 4 A supply all
   day.
8. Look: choose bare, smoke or opal (+ gap) deliberately and say why.
   Smoked acrylic is the classic finish on HUB75 panels.
9. Real size: the pixels are 2 mm apart and the whole face is 128 x 128
   mm, seen from 1-2 m. Judge legibility at --scale 3 (about true size on a
   monitor) as well as zoomed.
10. Technical:
    - frame() is a pure function of time: closed form, or deterministic
      caches keyed by the minute. If you must simulate, warmup <= 2 s.
    - Dim with ctx.night.
    - `node tools/check.mjs --design ramen` has no FAIL.
    - Speed: frame() mean under ~1.5 ms in Node for these 4096 pixels. Draw into typed arrays, no per-pixel allocation, no
      per-pixel closures in hot loops. The live view must hold 60 fps.
    - No hidden extra features.

PROCESS
1. Pitch three very different concepts for this layout. Pick the strongest,
   judging harshly.
2. Digits first. Render every digit in every slot:
   node tools/render.mjs ramen --clocks 00:00:30,11:11:30,22:22:30,03:33:30,04:44:30,05:55:30,16:06:30,17:17:30,18:18:30,19:39:30 --out out/ramen-digits.png
   Iterate until each digit is beautiful and unmistakable.
3. Build, then render these, and look at every image:
   - out/ramen.png
   - out/ramen-turn.png: --clock 10:09:58 --frames 9 --duration 6 --sheet
   - out/ramen-hour.png: the same at --clock 10:59:58, and once at 23:59:58
   - out/ramen.gif: --clock 10:09:57 --frames 150 --duration 6 --gif --scale 4
   - out/ramen-hour.gif (same settings at 10:59:57)
4. Critique it in the owner's voice. Is the font, layout, motion or
   transition ordinary? Is there a wow moment? Fix the weakest part and
   re-render. At least two rounds.
5. Report:
   - the idea in one sentence
   - the look chosen and why
   - render paths
   - the check.mjs result
   - the honest weakest point

LESSONS FROM ROUNDS 1-7 (93 faces; the owner called them all amazing)
- The most common weakness: at rest the face still reads as four plain
  digits with a themed background, and the wow lives only in the turns. Make
  the time at rest belong to the theme too: carried by objects, characters,
  light or material, or by a non-digit system (dials, counts, positions)
  that still reads clearly.
- Characters smaller than about 5 LEDs read only while they move. Make the
  heroes big enough, or let motion carry them.
- Thin one-pixel digits get lost on busy grounds, so leave a dark margin.
- Round 3 on this panel: most faces still read at rest as 2x2 digits placed
  in a scene. The strongest stills (inkwell, atelier, voxel, pinball,
  snowglobe) made the time part of the world's material. Try a composition
  that is not a 2x2 block of equal digits.
- Fixes I had to make after round 3, check yours for the same: a character's
  limb resting over a digit made 0 read as 6; a moonlit night state made the
  hours vanish; a bright noon sky washed the digits out; a 4 read as U.
  Render all ten digits AND a night clock (e.g. 02:47) and a noon clock.
- Round 4 on this panel: the strongest still was zensand (the time carved
  into a material, lit by a raking light). The weakest at rest were a
  perspective view that shrank the far digits until a 1 vanished, and gear
  digits whose 1 read as a cross: check every digit at --scale 3. Several
  agents judged motion only from stills; contact sheets at 9 frames hide
  pops, so also render a dense sheet (--frames 25) of each transition.
- Round 5 (assemblies): the strongest was foldbox, which used the
  assembly's SHAPE as the idea (a plus is the net of a box that folds into
  a 3D cube on the hour). Fixes I had to make after round 5:
  - characters at 13-22 px read as blobs: draw heroes bold and simple
    (16+ px, outlined, a few flat colours, big clear eyes) and check them
    at --scale 3;
  - extra times on the face (a typed log of past minutes, departure times)
    competed with the real time: anything else that looks like a time must
    be clearly secondary (much fainter, smaller);
  - an hour show that hid the minutes for 6 s: the full time must stay
    readable through the hour (brief gaps only);
  - small heroes and scenery living below 32/255 vanish on 3+ panel chains
    (the dim end is crushed): keep what matters above ~32/255;
  - squat digit cuts (24x32) made 9 read as 'a': test every digit in its
    real slot size.
- Round 6 fixes:
  - background made of the same stuff as the digits (garbage blocks under
    block digits, stitches round stitch digits) competed with the time:
    keep the ground quiet, give each numeral a dark moat or a keyline, and
    one hue for the time;
  - a non-digit display (planet positions) read only once it had bold
    markers and a visible track for the minutes: if the time is a
    position, draw the scale it is read against;
  - a tilted 3D view needs enough tilt to read the plan (about 25 deg+).
- Big assemblies (6-9 panels) run a HUB75 chain per row, each on its own
  controller kept in step; the raster is still one picture. Each extra
  panel is more pixels to fill: budget frame() as stated above, and make
  the size pay off (things that need room: long flights, crowds, maps).
- Round 7: the strongest stills (trainset, tvwall, candles, clawmachine)
  put the time on a big, bold physical object of the theme (wagon cargo,
  TV sets, wax candles, plush toys) with the world alive around it.
  Fixes I had to make after round 7:
  - ctx.out is linear light and the eye is not: 13/255 linear looks about
    a third as bright as full, not a twentieth. A "dim" secondary scale at
    13/255 still read as strongly as the lit time. Anything meant to be
    faint needs ~2-4/255; judge brightness ratios by eye on the render;
  - any numeral-sized ring or stroke shape near the digits reads as a
    digit (a stadium oval made 10:09 read "100"): nothing beside the time
    may share its size, stroke and lighting;
  - an hour lap that ran the digits backwards showed "95" for half a
    second: every frame counts, so check dense sheets across the hour;
  - an effect that changes over the minute (a pour's heavy start, foam
    spreading) closed a 9's counter late in the minute: check stills at
    :05, :30 and :55, not only :30;
  - in V8, objects of inconsistent shape in hot loops doubled frame time
    after a few minutes of running: keep hot objects monomorphic (same
    fields, same order, same types).
- Round 8 (12 faces): the strongest (shadowplay, mahjong, griddle,
  projector) put the time on a big physical object of the theme. Fixes:
  a drawing machine invisible at rest (show the mechanism); time too small
  and dim in a big banner (the time must use the space); a 0 built as a box
  read as a box with a 1 inside (allow counters and holes).
- Round 9 (8 faces): rpgbattle (wyrm-coiled hour, bouncing damage-number
  minutes), magician, matryoshka and abyss were strongest. Fixes I had to
  make: four equal upright digits on one baseline with the theme bolted on
  (dragondance first pass: make the object carry the digits, vary height
  and tilt); a 2x2 of thin outline digits in a faint "pretty stone" (geode:
  the object must be bold and the digits grow from it); a whole panel left
  nearly empty (forge's bottom third); hours-over-minutes at rest is still
  the most common default: find a composition the theme suggests.
- Don't add the owner's avatar or any recurring mascot: the owner never
  asked for one. Characters, if any, belong to the theme.
