Design ONE clock face for a monochrome 096-128x64 OLED with the oled-clock skill
(.claude/skills/oled-clock). Read SKILL.md and references/gotchas.md first.
Edit only sim/designs/wiper.js (already registered as a placeholder; give it a
real name and blurb). Don't touch other designs, index.js or sim/js/, and don't
render other designs.

Starting idea (push past it, or replace it if you find something stronger):
A car windshield in the rain at night. Raindrops land and run on the glass with real droplet physics (drops merge, grow, and streak down), and the drops gather into the numerals, as if the time condenses out of the rain (design droplet numerals: beaded strokes of round, shaded water drops with a bright highlight each, no speckle; small drops merge into strokes). Street lights and traffic lights beyond blur into bokeh discs. Minute turn: the wiper blade sweeps a real arc round its pivot, the rubber blade flexing, a sheet of water pushed ahead of it, clearing the old digit, and the rain rebuilds the new one behind it. Hour: a car passes, its headlights flare through every drop, the wipers go double speed.

GOAL: a stranger looks at it and says "wait, that's a clock?" Creativity comes
first. A competent, pretty, conventional face is a fail. The bar to beat is
scope, cube, numberfolk, nixie and urwerk.

REQUIREMENTS
1. Concept: the time is expressed by the theme's own mechanism. Test: if
   swapping your digits for a plain bitmap font still works, the digits are not
   part of the idea yet.
2. Letterforms: draw your own, made of or carried by the theme's material. No
   kit fonts (font5x7, font3x5, strokeDigit, segDigit, dotText) for the time.
   Design all ten digits as a set. Any date or label speaks the same visual
   language, or leave it out.
3. Layout: never four digits in a row, with or without decoration around
   them; that is the default every weak face falls back to. Use a scene,
   depth, a dial, a mechanism, hours and minutes as different systems.
4. Always alive: smooth 60 fps sub-pixel motion that belongs to the theme,
   every second, not only at the turn.
5. Minute turn: the old digit physically becomes the new one through the
   theme's process, 0.8-3 s, eased, no pops or cuts. Never show a wrong time.
   The hour gets a bigger, distinct show.
6. Composition: one hero, one or two supporting elements, a designed
   background, plenty of black. It must read at 1x: no tiny glyphs, no noise
   as texture. Every element belongs to the same world.
7. Characters, if any, are carefully hand-drawn (sprites beat formulas at this
   size) and interact with the time.
8. Technical: frame() is a pure function of time; `node tools/check.mjs
   --design wiper` passes; frame() under ~2 ms. No hidden extra features.

PROCESS
1. Pitch three very different concepts, none like an existing design
   (`node tools/render.mjs --list`). Pick the strongest, judging harshly.
2. Digits first: render out/wiper-digits.png and iterate until every digit is
   beautiful on its own.
3. Build, then render out/wiper.png, out/wiper-turn.png (--sheet across
   10:09:58), out/wiper-hour.png (--sheet across 10:59:58), out/wiper.gif and
   out/wiper-hour.gif. Look at every image.
4. Ask: is the font, layout, motion or transition ordinary? Is it fascinating?
   Fix the weakest part and re-render. At least two rounds.
5. Report: the idea in one sentence, render paths, the check.mjs result, and
   the honest weakest point.

Lessons from rounds 10-13 (32 faces), from the owner's gallery:
- Strongest stills last round: ropeknot (3D laid-rope numerals hanging from
  a yard), spintops (numbers painted on spinning tippe tops), cootie (paper
  fortune teller, hour in the mouth, minutes on the pockets), stringart.
- Fixes I had to make last round, check yours for the same:
  - a lit, dithered surface everywhere (paper, slab, lattice) made the time
    unfindable: the time must be the brightest, boldest thing, on plenty of
    black, with a black moat round each stroke;
  - an hour shown as a pointer with no scale could not be read: if the time
    is a position, draw the scale it is read against;
  - an hour cut into a shaded slab got lost in the shading;
  - 12 h with no AM/PM cue (18:56 read as 6): use 24 h, or a clear cue;
  - minute digits made of a grid of small balls/cells read as a lumpy grid.
- Still true: textured plain letterforms are not enough, the letterform is
  designed for the theme; no speckle (scattered single pixels) as shading or
  texture; solid contour lines beat hatching; the hour show keeps the time
  readable (blank for more than ~1 s is a fail); never four digits in a row
  (compose a scene); characters and props at 1x are bold, simple
  silhouettes. If the face is 3D, look at sim/designs/cube.js: real geometry,
  perspective, shading by facing, correct occlusion, always moving. Keep
  frame() under ~2 ms.
